- Video and audio move between your own devices over your local network. They are not uploaded to the internet.

- LanCast's web browser works like any browser: the websites you visit receive your visits and can set cookies, which stay on your phone. LanCast does not see or keep your browsing.
- When a Windows PC is mirrored to a phone or tablet and you allow control, the touches and typing on that phone or tablet are sent to the PC over your local network. They are not recorded.

4. INFORMATION STORED ON YOUR DEVICE

LanCast stores the following only on the device where you installed it. It never leaves that device except as described in section 5.

(a) Library and playlists. The titles, folder names, durations, sizes and dates of the video and music files that LanCast lists, and the playlists you create. Playlists are stored in the app's private storage.

(b) Screens you have used. The name and network address of each screen you chose, so that you do not have to choose it again. You can remove them at any time from Settings, then "Forget saved screens".

(c) PIN lock settings. Whether the PIN lock is on, and the PIN itself, for each screen where you turned it on. On Android the PIN is stored in the app's private storage. On Windows it is stored in a file named settings.json in your Windows user profile (AppData\Roaming\LanCast). Anyone with access to an unlocked device can read it; see section 9.

(d) Pro status. A record that LanCast Pro is unlocked on this device, so that the app can work offline after a purchase.

(e) Information held only in memory. While mirroring, the picture and sound are encoded and sent straight to your screen and are not stored. While the app is running, LanCast keeps the most recent file-server events and playback statistics, to show them on your screen. They are discarded when the app closes. Thumbnails of your files are also kept in memory only.

(f) Windows only. The PIN lock settings in (c), and a firewall rule added by the Microsoft Store package. The rule allows incoming LanCast connections on private networks only. It does not allow connections on public networks.

(g) Web browser data. Cookies, site storage and the cache of the pages you open in LanCast's browser, kept by Android System WebView in the app's private storage. LanCast keeps no list of the pages you visit.

You can delete all of this at any time by uninstalling LanCast (Android and Windows), or by clearing the app's data in your device settings. Uninstalling also removes the playlists, saved screens and PIN settings.

5. INFORMATION THAT TRAVELS OVER YOUR NETWORK

When you cast a file, LanCast sends the file, and short control messages such as play, pause and seek, between the phone or PC that holds the file and the screen you chose. Messages and files are sent only to devices on the same local network: private addresses such as 192.168.x.x, 10.x.x.x and 172.16.x.x to 172.31.x.x, and IPv6 local addresses. The app refuses to connect to any address outside your local network, with one exception: when you share a web video with LanCast, your phone (never the screen) downloads it from the website and passes it on.

This traffic has these properties, which you should know:
- It travels between your devices. It is not sent to us or to any other company. We operate no servers that receive it.
- LanCast does not encrypt it. Other devices on the same network, and anyone able to monitor that network, could in principle see the file or the control messages. Use LanCast on networks you trust, such as your home network, and turn on the PIN lock on shared or public networks.
- With the PIN lock on, the PIN itself is never sent over the network. Phones send a proof that expires within about two minutes, and after five wrong guesses further attempts are blocked for 30 seconds. This protects the controls, but not the video itself.
- To find televisions and speakers that accept "Play To", your phone sends a standard discovery message (SSDP, on UDP port 1900) to your local network, and reads the name and controls each device describes. It then sends that device the play, pause, seek and volume commands you choose, and the address of the file on your phone.
- Screens announce their name on your local network (using mDNS on UDP port 5353) so that phones can find them. Anyone on the same network can see that a LanCast screen exists and what it is called.

On Windows, LanCast reads your PC's Wi-Fi signal strength by running the built-in Windows command "netsh wlan show interfaces". It uses only the signal percentage. It does not read or record the network name or other network details. The percentage is shown in the live stats on the phone that is controlling the screen, on your local network only. It is never sent to LanCast or anyone else.

7. PERMISSIONS AND WHY LANCAST USES THEM

Android:
- Photos and videos, music and audio (READ_MEDIA_VIDEO, READ_MEDIA_AUDIO): to list and play your files.
- Selected photos and videos (READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED, Android 14 and later): if you allow access only to some files, LanCast uses only those.
- Storage (READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, Android 12 and earlier): the same purpose, on older devices.
- Internet and Wi-Fi state (INTERNET, ACCESS_WIFI_STATE): to connect to other devices on your network and read your Wi-Fi signal strength. LanCast does not use location permission for this.
- Keep the device awake and media-playback foreground service (WAKE_LOCK, FOREGROUND_SERVICE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK): so playback continues when the screen is off. A notification appears while a file is being sent.
- Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS): to show that a file is being sent.
- Screen capture and media-projection foreground service (FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PROJECTION): only while you mirror your screen. Android asks you each time and shows that sharing is on.
- Record audio (RECORD_AUDIO): only while mirroring, to capture the sound other apps play so it can play on your screen. LanCast never records the microphone. Apps that block capture are not heard.

Windows: a firewall rule for private networks (TCP port 8765 for control messages, TCP port 8767 for a mirrored phone screen, UDP port 5353 for discovery), and the privateNetworkClientServer capability. When you show the PC on another screen, LanCast captures that display and the sound the PC plays (never the microphone) only until you stop; if you allow control, it moves the mouse and types on the PC for the phone or tablet showing it. No other system access is requested.

You can withdraw any permission at any time in your device or Windows settings. LanCast will still open, but the related feature will stop working.

- Windows runtime and packaging components run on your PC.
- Google Sites hosts this web page. As the host, Google may process visitor information such as IP addresses and cookies under its own policies. LanCast adds no analytics or tracking to this page.

Open-source licences and the source-code offer are shown in the app (Settings, then Licences and credits) and in the Windows program folder as THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt.

9. SECURITY

LanCast has no server, so your files and playback messages do not sit in a database that could be breached. The main risks are on the network and on the devices themselves:
- Traffic between devices is not encrypted (section 5). Use trusted networks.
- Anyone who can open your phone or PC can read the saved PIN and the names of your screens. Protect your devices with a screen lock.
- With the PIN lock off, any device on the same network can play, pause or stop what a screen shows. Turn the PIN lock on whenever the network is not fully trusted.

We take reasonable steps to keep LanCast's code secure, including checks that refuse non-local connections and the PIN-proof system. No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a security problem that affects you, we will tell you and fix it as soon as reasonably possible.
